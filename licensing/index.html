    Licensing & Support

    Philter Desktop is made by Philterd, the company behind the Phileas redaction engine that powers it. This page explains how the free and open-source software and the paid official product fit together.

    The short version

    • The software is free and open source. The source code is published under the Apache License, Version 2.0, so you (or your IT department) can inspect exactly what it does, and anyone may use, modify, and build it.
    • The official build is free for personal use and evaluation. You can download the official, signed build from philterd.ai and use it at no charge for personal use or to try it out.
    • Work use requires a subscription. Using the official build for work (your job, your clients, or an organization, even if you work alone) needs a per-user subscription ($199 per user, per year), with an active subscription for each person who uses it that way. The subscription also includes support and maintained updates.
    • "Philter" is a trademark of Philterd. Anyone may build the open-source code, but the official builds are distributed under the Philter brand by Philterd.
    • For the current, definitive pricing and terms, see philterd.ai.

    Reading the license agreement in the app

    You can read the licenses at any time from inside Philter Desktop: go to Help → About and click View licenses. It shows both the Philterd Commercial License Agreement (the EULA) and the Apache License 2.0 for the open-source code, and works offline. The same About box has Third-party notices and Third-party licenses links: the first lists the open-source components Philter Desktop includes, and the second has the full text of each one's license. The installation folder also contains LICENSE.txt, NOTICE.txt, and THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt.

    When you need a subscription

    The official, signed build is free to download, and free for personal use and evaluation. You need a per-user subscription ($199 per user, per year, from philterd.ai) to use the official build for work, with an active subscription for each person who does so.

    • Personal use (free): redacting your own documents, or your household's, outside of any work.
    • Evaluation (free): trying Philter Desktop to decide whether to buy it. There is no time limit, but evaluation ends once you start using Philter Desktop, or the redacted files it produces, in your work, such as sending them to a client, filing them with a court, or delivering them to a customer.
    • Work use (subscription): anything connected to your job, your business, or your clients, patients, or customers, or done for an organization, including nonprofits and government. This applies even if you work alone and on your own computer.

    For example:

    Situation License needed?
    Redacting your own medical records before sending them to an insurer No, personal use
    Testing Philter Desktop on sample files before deciding to buy No, evaluation
    A solo attorney redacting client exhibits Yes
    A bookkeeper redacting client statements from home Yes
    An employee redacting files for their job on a personal laptop Yes

    The full definitions are in section 4.6 of the Philterd Commercial License Agreement. The agreement has a version number at the top. When a new version of Philter Desktop comes with a newer agreement, Philter Desktop shows it once the first time you open the new version, so you can review and accept the updated terms. The subscription permits work use and includes:

    • Support: a direct line to the people who build Philter Desktop, for setup, policy, or workflow questions. Email support@philterd.ai.
    • Maintained releases: ongoing updates, fixes, and improvements.

    The Apache license covers the source code: anyone may inspect, modify, and build it, and a technical user is always free to build and run their own copy under that license. What it does not provide is support or the right to use Philterd's Philter trademark and branding. This is the common commercial open-source model: the source is open for transparency, and commercial subscriptions to the official product sustain it.

    To subscribe, renew, or ask about pricing for a team or firm, visit philterd.ai.

    Entering your work license

    There's no license key to type. Instead, Philter Desktop confirms your purchase with the email address you bought it with:

    1. Choose Help → Manage License….
    2. Enter your purchase email and click Send Code. If that address has a purchase, a code is emailed to it within a minute or so.
    3. Enter the code and click Verify.

    The window then says Licensed for work use, with your email and the date your license runs through, and the About box shows the same. While your computer is online, Philter Desktop checks the license with philterd.ai about once a month, so a renewal is picked up automatically. If your license ends or is refunded, it's removed from the computer at the next check; nothing in Philter Desktop is ever locked either way.

    A computer that's never online: sign in at account.philterd.ai on another computer, click Download license file, copy the file across, and use Import a license file… in the same window.

    A license covers the number of people you bought it for. Philter Desktop doesn't count or enforce seats; please enter it only on computers used by people your purchase covers.

    A note for IT departments

    Philter Desktop runs entirely on the user's machine and stores its data locally (encrypted; see Settings → Security), so there is no server to stand up, no cloud account to manage, and no document data leaving your environment. Because the code is open source, your security team can confirm that for themselves rather than trusting a sealed black box. The official signed installer supports both a per-user installation (no administrator rights required) and an all-users installation; see Getting Started. For deployment or support questions, contact Philterd through philterd.ai.

    Network use. Philter Desktop makes two kinds of network request, both over HTTPS. The first is an update check: when its window first opens, about once a day while it runs in the background, and from Help → Check for Updates…. The check is an HTTPS download of https://philterd.ai/philterdesktop.json, a small file listing the latest version. The request's User-Agent header is PhilterDesktop/<version> (for example, PhilterDesktop/1.4.0), which philterd.ai's web server logs, along with the computer's IP address, so Philterd can count the versions in use. These logs are deleted after about 90 days. No documents, identifiers, or other usage data are sent. To turn automatic checks off for every user on a machine, set this registry value (users can still choose Help → Check for Updates… themselves):

    HKEY_LOCAL_MACHINE\SOFTWARE\Policies\PhilterDesktop
    DisableAutoUpdateCheck  (DWORD) = 1

    The same value under HKEY_CURRENT_USER turns checks off for one user.

    The second is the work license check, and only on computers where someone has entered a license (see Entering your work license). Entering one sends the purchase email, and then the emailed code, to https://account.philterd.ai/api/desktop/license/. After that, about once a month while online, Philter Desktop sends its signed license token to the same address to pick up renewals. The account site keeps a log of these events, with the IP address and the email of the purchaser, for a year, for support. A computer with no license entered never contacts it. The license check happens on any licensed computer that's online, however the license was entered (including from a file), and the DisableAutoUpdateCheck policy doesn't turn it off. A computer that never goes online can still be licensed with a license file; the monthly check then simply never happens.

    License acceptance. Each Windows user accepts the license agreement the first time they open Philter Desktop, and again once for each new version. The setup program does not ask for acceptance, so silent and managed installs need no extra steps.

    Important reminder about redacted documents

    Whatever your arrangement, keep the most important point in mind: automated redaction uses statistical and machine-learning methods, and no automated tool can be guaranteed to catch everything. Every redacted document should be reviewed by a person before it is shared or relied upon. This is why Philter Desktop names its output files with the word draft by default. Support from Philterd can help you configure the tool well, but the final review is always yours.
